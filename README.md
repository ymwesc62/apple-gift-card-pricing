## What $5 actually buys

Apple sells this card in fixed denominations: **$5, $10, $15, $25, $50 and $100** in USD, and the same six tiers in CAD for Canadian accounts. The $5 tier is a top-up, not a budget. It covers apps and games from the App Store, in-app purchases, subscriptions to Apple Music, Apple TV+, Apple Arcade and iCloud+, films and music from iTunes, Apple Books, and hardware or accessories from the Apple Store, though some physical products are excluded from gift card payment.

Two details matter before you add it to a cart. The card is **digital only**, delivered as a 16-digit code you scratch or reveal on screen, and it is **region-locked** to the country it was issued for. A "US $5" code is not a universal top-up. If you were searching for a $5 iTunes gift card, that product no longer exists as a separate line; Apple folded the old App Store & iTunes card into the single Apple Gift Card, so you are buying the same thing under a new name.

## Where the extra $2.99 goes

PCGameSupply is the only seller in this set that itemises the gap. Its $5 card costs $5.53, and the page says that figure "includes all applicable processing and digital delivery fees" with no further charge at checkout. That is the shape of the markup across the market: a card reseller absorbs a fixed per-transaction cost plus a percentage on every order, so a $5 ticket carries a proportionally heavy load. The distance between the cheapest verified price ($5.00) and the highest ($7.99) is **$2.99, or roughly 60% of the card's face value**, and none of that extra money reaches your Apple balance.

Which makes the $5 tier the worst denomination to overpay on. Whatever a seller adds above face value gets charged again on the next card, so three $5 cards bought at a $1 markup cost $3 more than the same $15 bought as a single code at face value. If you know you need more than $5, buy the larger denomination and skip the small-ticket premium.

## Region lock: the mistake that costs you the full $5

Every seller checked says the same thing in slightly different words: valid for United States Apple accounts only, and the Apple Account region must be set to the United States. Giftsy spells out the consequence, stating that an incorrect region selection is not refundable. You do not get a working code and a refund; you get a dead code and a support ticket.

So check your account region before paying, not after. Open your Apple Account settings and look at the country or region listed there. If it says anything other than United States, a US card will not redeem. Canadian readers need the CAD version, which ScratchMonkeys stocks alongside the US one; everyone else needs the card that matches their own account country.

One more limit that catches people mid-purchase: a gift card balance cannot be combined with Apple Pay or PayPal on Apple's online store, and an order there cannot be split across several credit cards. On the App Store, if a purchase costs more than your balance, the remainder goes to your default payment method.

## Refunds, lost codes, and what "all sales final" means

Every seller in this market sells digital codes on the same terms. ScratchMonkeys states "All sales are final"; Giftsy says codes are non-refundable once delivered; PCGameSupply says non-refundable after redemption; Gameflip says no refunds once the code is delivered and redeemed successfully. The card is also **single-use and not reloadable**; when the $5 is spent, you buy a new card rather than topping the old one up.

The harder limit is what happens after the code is revealed. PCGameSupply puts it plainly: codes cannot be re-issued once revealed. Lose the confirmation email, delete the SMS, or paste the code into a chat window and you have no recovery path through the seller. Apple's own US gift card terms, last updated 14 October 2024, add that neither the issuer nor Apple is responsible for lost or stolen cards, and that the sole remedy for a nonfunctional card is replacement. Keep the confirmation email and screenshot the code until the balance shows in your account. And never share it: Giftsy's warning is blunt, that anyone asking for your code is likely running a scam.

One thing you *can* do before redeeming is give the card away. It is transferable as a gift while unredeemed. Once it is redeemed to an Apple ID, the balance belongs to that account and cannot be moved to another one, so send it to the right person first.

## Does a $5 Apple Gift Card expire? Two answers, one safe move

The sellers disagree, and none of them is Apple. Giftsy's FAQ says the card must be redeemed within one year of purchase, though the credit itself does not expire after redemption. ScratchMonkeys says Apple Gift Cards have no expiration date and stay valid until the full amount is used; PCGameSupply says US Apple Gift Cards "generally do not expire." Apple's own US gift card terms do not state a redemption deadline in the sections available to check.

Given the split, treat the shorter window as the real one. Redeem within a few weeks of buying, and the question never comes up. Sitting on a $5 code for eighteen months to save it for a rainy day buys you nothing and risks the one-year reading being correct.
